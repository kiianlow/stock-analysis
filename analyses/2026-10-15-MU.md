## Verdict

Micron is a far stronger business today than at any point in its history, and every trailing number says it is getting stronger, not just bigger. The price already assumes it stays that way. At $914 the market pays for my bull case, where the memory boom runs through fiscal 2029 and mid-cycle earnings settle near $59 a share. Six early cycle-peak signals are firing. Great company at an expensive price, which is the exact trap the framework is built to stop.

## Thesis (facts unless labelled)

Micron sells DRAM, high-bandwidth memory (HBM) and NAND. AI data centres need far more memory per server, and HBM uses about three times the wafer capacity of DDR5 per gigabyte, which pulled supply away from everything else. Fiscal 2026 (ended 3 Sept 2026) revenue rose 256% to $133.19B with a 74.6% GAAP operating margin. Fiscal Q4 revenue was $54.23B at an 86.8% gross margin, and Q1 FY27 is guided to $61.5B. Micron signed 26 strategic customer agreements expected to be over 35% of revenue through 2030, holds $32B of customer commitments mostly as cash prepayments, and says over 75% of FY27 output is secured. Management expects conditions to be tighter in FY27 and FY28 than in FY26 (company statement, an estimate). Net cash is $68.3B.

## Bear case

- Supply is coming. Micron plans over $50B of FY27 capex versus $27.4B net in FY26. Samsung and SK Hynix plans are reported between $575B and $1.3T. New Micron fabs start in early to mid 2027. Memory has never avoided oversupply after a spending wave like this.
- Pricing momentum is fading. DRAM contract increases went from about 90% a quarter (Q1 2026) to 50 to 60% (Q2), 13 to 18% (Q3) and a forecast 10 to 15% (Q4). Q1 FY27 gross margin is guided below Q4, the first sequential dip of this upcycle.
- Demand depends on AI capex that is increasingly debt-funded. Hyperscaler free cash flow is under strain. PC and phone makers are already cutting memory content.
- History. From FY2010 to FY2025 average net income was 24% of the peak year. The stock fell 98% after its 2000 peak and did not regain it until January 2022. It fell 49% in 2022 and 54% in 2024 to 2025.
- The price needs the bull case. The reverse DCF needs free cash flow to grow 9.6% a year for ten years from a record base. Free cash flow averaged $1.3B a year from FY2010 to FY2025.

## Valuation (Gate 8)

- Trailing P/E 12.3 on GAAP EPS $74.33. Forward P/E 5.2 (FY27) and 4.5 (FY28). For a memory maker, a low P/E at record earnings is the warning, not the opportunity.
- FCF yield 5.6% on FY26 FCF against a 5.21% 10-year yield. Barely above risk-free, at what is likely near-peak cash flow.
- Cost of equity 12.7% (risk-free 5.21%, beta 1.5 assumed, equity risk premium 5%). Beta from 1.2 to 1.8 moves the base value only from $527 to $506.
- Reverse DCF: $914 needs FY26 free cash flow of $59B to grow 9.6% a year for 10 years. Equivalent to about $92B of free cash flow a year forever.
- Implied mid-cycle EPS: on the base-case timeline, $914 needs normalised EPS of about $59, roughly 80% of FY26's record. History says mid-cycle earnings run near a quarter of peak.
- Expected annual return to the weighted value: -22.3% a year over 2 years, -15.5% a year over 3 years. Hurdle is 10.21% (10-year yield plus 5 points). Fails.

Method (framework 8.8, scripts/valuation.py cyclical): discounted boom-year FCF per share, plus mid-cycle earnings x 15 reached when the boom ends, plus $59.55 net cash per share. Tax 15% (FY26 effective rate 14.8%). Assumptions and probabilities are my estimates.

## Balance sheet and earnings quality (Gates 5 and 6)

- Net cash $68.3B, debt $5.18B, interest coverage above 900x, Altman Z 14.6 (safe), Piotroski 7 of 8.
- Operating cash flow 1.06x net income. Accruals ratio -3.4% of average assets. Cash earnings exceed reported earnings.
- Beneish M is -0.32, above the -1.78 line, but only because of sales growth (SGI 3.56). With sales growth neutralised the score is -2.61, below both lines. Treated as a growth artefact, not a kill switch. Three Beneish inputs (AQI, DEPI, SGAI) defaulted to neutral because the feed lacks PP&E and SG&A.
- Feed errors caught: quarterly capex repeats exactly in two quarter pairs, and the four quarters sum to $35.77B against the 10-K's $30.71B. Annual figures used. The feed also excludes $35.1B of short and long-term marketable investments from cash, so net cash comes from the 10-K.

## Management and insiders (Gate 7)

- Execution is strong: HBM ramp, customised HBM for Nvidia's next GPU, record guidance beaten twice in a row.
- Capital allocation: $10.0B of debt repaid and only $650M of buybacks at peak prices, which is sensible. FY27 capex over $50B is the main concern. Cyclicals that add capacity at the peak usually regret it.
- Insiders sold $373.9M in 12 months, CEO $200.0M. Sales are noisy, but there has been no buying since one director in January at $337.
